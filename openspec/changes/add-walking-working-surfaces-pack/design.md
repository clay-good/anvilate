## 29 CFR 1910.29 (fall protection systems and falling object protection, criteria)

- (b)(1): top edge height 42 in (107 cm) ± 3 in (8 cm) above the walking-working surface;
  may exceed 45 in (114 cm) if every other criterion of (b) is met. **Confirmed**: the
  first draft had this from a state fact sheet and flagged it for confirmation.
- (b)(3): the guardrail system withstands without failure at least 200 lbf (890 N) applied
  downward or outward within 2 in (5 cm) of the top edge, **at any point along the top
  rail**.
- (b)(4): under the 200 lbf downward load the top rail must not deflect to a height of
  less than 39 in (99 cm) above the walking-working surface.
- (b)(5): midrails, screens, mesh, intermediate vertical members, solid panels and
  equivalent intermediate members withstand at least 150 lbf (667 N) in any downward or
  outward direction at any point.
- (f)(7): handrails and stair-rail top rails withstand at least 200 lbf (890 N) in any
  downward or outward direction within 2 in of any point along the top edge.
- (f): stair rail systems installed on or after January 17, 2017 are at least 42 in
  (107 cm) from the leading edge of the tread to the top surface of the top rail.
- (k)(1): toeboards are at least 3.5 in (9 cm) high measured from the top edge to the
  walking-working surface (2.5 in around vehicle repair, service or assembly pits), and
  withstand at least 50 lbf (222 N) in any downward or outward direction at any point.
  **Read on the re-read**; the first draft left it open.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.29

## 29 CFR 1910.25 (stairways)

- (b)(2): vertical clearance above any stair tread to any overhead obstruction at least
  6 ft 8 in (203 cm) from the leading edge of the tread.
- (b)(3): uniform riser heights and tread depths between landings.
- (b)(4): landings and platforms at least the width of the stair and at least 30 in
  (76 cm) deep in the direction of travel.
- (b)(6): each stair supports at least five times the normal anticipated live load, and
  never less than a concentrated load of 1,000 lb (454 kg) applied at any point.
- (c) standard stairs: 30 to 50 degrees from the horizontal; riser at most 9.5 in (24 cm);
  tread depth at least 9.5 in; width at least 22 in (56 cm) between vertical barriers.
- (d) spiral: riser at most 9.5 in; headroom at least 6 ft 6 in.
- (e) ship stairs: slope 50 to 70 degrees; open risers with a vertical rise between tread
  surfaces of 6.5 to 12 in (17 to 30 cm); tread depth at least 4 in (10 cm); tread **width**
  at least 18 in (46 cm). **Corrected on the re-read**: the dimension at 18 in is the
  tread's width, and the first draft called it the stair's width.
- (f) alternating tread: slope 50 to 70 degrees; 17 to 24 in between handrails; tread
  depth at least 8.5 in (22 cm); open risers required where the tread depth is under
  9.5 in; tread width at least 7 in (18 cm) at the leading edge.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.25

## 29 CFR 1910.23 (ladders)

- (b)(2): rungs, steps and cleats spaced not less than 10 in (25 cm) and not more than
  14 in (36 cm) between centerlines.
- (b)(4): minimum clear width 16 in (41 cm) for fixed ladders, measured before a ladder
  safety system is installed (11.5 in for portable ladders).
- (d)(2): at least 7 in (18 cm) perpendicular from the rung centerline to the nearest
  permanent object behind (4.5 in for elevator pit ladders).
- (d)(4): side rails of through or side-step ladders extend at least 42 in (1.1 m) above
  the access level or landing platform.
- (d)(5): for through ladders the extension's side rails flare to not less than 24 in
  (61 cm) and not more than 30 in (76 cm) of clearance; at most 36 in (91 cm) with a
  ladder safety system.
- (d)(12): step-across distance 7 to 12 in (18 to 30 cm) for through ladders, 15 to 20 in
  (38 to 51 cm) for side-step ladders.
- (d)(13): without a cage or well, at least 15 in (38 cm) clear each side of the ladder
  centerline and at least 30 in (76 cm) perpendicular on the climbing side — reducible to
  24 in (61 cm) at an unavoidable obstruction with deflector plates.
- (d)(1): fixed ladders support their maximum intended load; **the text states no number**,
  so the load is the document's to declare.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.23

## 29 CFR 1910.28 (duty to have fall protection)

- (b)(1)(i): an unprotected side or edge 4 ft (1.2 m) or more above a lower level requires
  fall protection.
- (b)(9): a fixed ladder extending **more than** 24 ft (7.3 m) above a lower level requires
  a personal fall arrest system, a ladder safety system, a cage or a well.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.28

A document declares one regime. The pack refuses to apply 1910 numbers to a document
declaring construction, and vice versa.

## The post base on anchors (task 3.1, first half)

The guardrail screen checks the post, the rails and the toeboard. It did not check what
holds the post down, and that is where the clause's 200 lbf ends up: as a moment at the
base, and as tension in the anchors. `anvilate.analysis.anchorage` answers it, and
`examples/guardrail_post_base_anchors.py` is the worked case — **3,098 N into each of two
anchors** of a 150 mm pattern, with the post's own weight resisting 2.4% of the moment.

The sharpest part is the two-bolt base plate: **along** the load two bolts take double the
four-bolt figure, because only one is ever in tension, and **across** it they are refused
— two anchors on one line resist no moment about that line. The same plate works or is a
mechanism depending on which way it is turned, and nothing on the drawing says which.

## Why the drawable run is blocked (task 3.1, second half)

A guardrail run of tube posts and tube rails cannot be expressed as a combination today,
for two reasons that are both checkable rather than matters of taste:

1. **The `tube` element builds along Z.** In a combination a tube can therefore be a post
   but not a horizontal rail seated on posts: a tube's `bottom` face is its *end cap*, so
   `face_to_face(rail.bottom, post.top)` stacks the rail on the post end to end. A mate
   set that lays the rail horizontal beside a post does exist — `face_to_face(front,
   right)` with two `edge_flush` mates — but then no mate places a **second post on the
   ground** at the rail's far end: every placement the vocabulary allows puts that post
   *on top of* the rail. A search over all 46,656 three-mate face combinations found four
   solutions and all four were that one.
2. **The alternative, `beam_member`, takes a catalogue section**, and the bundled profile
   table holds **only W shapes** — no HSS, no pipe, no CHS. A round-tube rail has no
   designation to name.

So the run needs one of: a tube that can be declared along an axis other than Z, a mate
that places a part at a declared offset from another without touching it, or hollow
sections in the profile table. The portal-frame combination already shows the pattern the
vocabulary *does* support — a horizontal member seated on two columns — so none of this is
a gap in combinations as such, only in what a guardrail is made of.
